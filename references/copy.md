# Interface copy and content

Use this when writing or reviewing text that helps someone understand or operate an interface. Match the product's voice and the user's knowledge. By default, write like a person (see Human voice below); an existing brand voice or the user's own style takes precedence.

## Write for the task

- Say what an item is, what happened, or what the user can do. Prefer concrete nouns and direct verbs over unsupported claims.
- Make action labels predict the result: “Save changes” is clearer than “Submit” when it saves changes. Keep the same action vocabulary through confirmation and feedback.
- Use labels and instructions that remain visible when a field is filled. Placeholders are examples, not labels.
- Explain errors in terms of what failed and the next recovery step. Empty states should explain what is absent and offer a relevant next action when one exists.
- Keep copy concise where space and scanning matter, but preserve context needed to make a safe or informed decision.

## Keep claims truthful

Do not invent customer counts, testimonials, certifications, awards, uptime, integrations, availability, or performance claims. Use supplied facts and link or label evidence where useful. In prototypes, clearly identify synthetic data and ensure it is consistent with the interface behavior.

Avoid vague marketing language when a product fact or outcome is available. Words such as “seamless,” “revolutionary,” or “effortless” are on the avoid-by-default list below; keep one only when it adds verifiable information instead of taking its place.

Avoid pseudo-technical jargon and robotic developer theater: do not label standard application features, case studies, or portfolio items with artificial phrases like `RUNTIME MATRIX`, `TAXONOMY BLUEPRINT`, `SENSOR TELEMETRY`, or `SYS SPEC INDEX`. Speak with natural human authority, clarity, and real engineering substance.

## Review in context

Read the page as the intended user. Check terminology consistency, sentence case where it matches the product, scanability, localization and text expansion, and whether errors, labels, units, dates, and numbers can be understood without visual styling. Preserve punctuation and tone choices when they fit the user's brief and do not harm clarity.

## Human voice (defaults)

Copy that sounds generated makes a good layout look cheap. Treat words as part of the design. Read each line aloud: would the person who made this say it to a friend who might use it? If not, rewrite. These are defaults for generated interface copy. Keep any item that the user's own brand voice or existing content already uses.

- **Say what it is and what it does.** "Waxed canvas, brass zip, fits a 16-inch laptop" beats "Elevate your everyday carry", as long as it is true for this product.
- **Specific over adjective.** A number the user supplied, a material, a place, a time. Never an invented one.
- **Short sentences, mixed with a few longer ones.** Do not write in chants. "Fast. Simple. Powerful." is a pattern, not a thought; use a list of three only when there are three real things.
- **Avoid em dashes** in interface copy. Use a period or a comma. They are one of the most recognizable marks of generated text.
- **Avoid "not just X, it's Y"**, rhetorical-question headings, and exclamation marks as enthusiasm.
- **Sentence case** for headings and buttons. No emoji as decoration.
- **Buttons are verbs that say what happens:** "Book a fitting", "Download the guide", "See the pricing". Not "Get started", "Learn more", or "Submit".

Words and phrases to avoid by default: unleash, supercharge, seamless, elevate, empower, leverage, harness, game-changer, cutting-edge, next-gen, revolutionize, streamline, robust, holistic, synergy, delve, tapestry, journey, unlock, reimagine, world-class, best-in-class, state-of-the-art, "in today's fast-paced world", "whether you're a ... or a ...", "say goodbye to", "welcome to", "discover", "crafted with passion", "take it to the next level", "all-in-one". If you keep one, be able to say why no plainer word works.

| Generated | Human |
|---|---|
| Elevate your everyday carry with our seamlessly crafted bags | A backpack that holds a 16-inch laptop, a lunch, and a rainy day |
| Unlock the power of your data | See every request that failed in the last hour |
| Get started today | Book a fitting |
| Our mission is to empower creators | We make the tools we wished we had when we started |
| Fast. Simple. Powerful. | Deploys in about a minute. Rolls back in one click. |
| Oops! Something went wrong | The payment did not go through. Check the card number and try again. |

When the real content is missing, write plausible, specific, realistic copy so the design can be judged fairly, and say in the handoff which lines are invented. Do not use lorem ipsum, "Feature one", "John Doe", or "Company Name". Aim for plain language a tired person on a phone can follow: short words, concrete nouns, one idea per sentence, and labels that name what a thing is rather than how the system implements it.
